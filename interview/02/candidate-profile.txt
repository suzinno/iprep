Candidate profile | Prime

Technical Stack & Data Architecture
    • Core Backend: Python, FastAPI, Uvicorn, Pydantic, SQLAlchemy, Alembic. They need to understand how to optimize synchronous FastAPI architecture for heavy enterprise loads 
    • Database Expertise (InterSystems IRIS): They will work with massive tables containing over 100 million records, meaning well-optimized SQL and extremely careful migrations are required. 
    • Message Brokering & Search: RabbitMQ, MQTT, Elasticsearch, ELK stack (Kibana, Elastic APM). The system handles millions of product attribute updates, which has previously caused memory overloads and crashed the production environment, so deep RabbitMQ expertise is a must 
    • Rigorous Testing Requirements: The client aims for 90% test coverage. The candidate should be an expert in Pytest and Xray, writing extensive unit and integration tests, and be patient enough to wait 20 to 30 minutes for CI/CD pipelines to pass.
    • DevOps & Infrastructure: Docker, Docker Compose, GitLab CI/CD, ArgoCD, GitOps, OpenShift, Prometheus.
    • Frontend Context: The candidate will closely collaborate with the frontend team, whose stack includes Javascript/Typescript, Vue, Nuxt, Pinia, VueUse, PrimeVue, Vite, and Playwright. Understanding OpenAPI/Orval for API contracts is essential.

Code Quality & Expectations AI Policy
    • Quality over Speed: On this project, quality and thoroughness are valued far more than speed. The client expects developers to carefully review test cases, cross-check requirements, and move cautiously.
    • AI Tooling: The client is becoming more open to using AI tools. There is a growing trend toward adopting IDE plugins and other AI solutions, while still keeping security and data protection in mind. But it does not mean that the reviews are skipped. Leads in teams review the code and are not happy with the fully AI generated code, so AI usage is very welcome, but all the code quality gates should be met and code should be human readable and clean.
    • Manual Self-Review: Technical leads explicitly do not trust code purely generated by AI. The developer is expected to thoroughly review their own work and fully understand the logic.
    • Code Linters & Security: Poetry, Mypy, Ruff, Black, isort, Pre-commit, SonarQube, and Trivy.

Workflow & Autonomy
    • Vague Requirements: Task descriptions from leads can be abstract and lack detail. The candidate cannot just sit and wait for perfect Jira tickets; instead - proactively schedule calls with Application Managers to reverse-engineer the business logic before coding
    • Broken Estimations: Initial task estimates are frequently inaccurate - a task estimated for 3 days can easily take a week. The candidate shouldn't stress about this. They just need to consistently update their "remaining estimate" in Jira every day and communicate blockers transparently.(Partially improved, but not perfect throughout the teams)
Soft Skills & Company Politics
    • Highly Political Environment: It’s common for some developers to publicly complain about the database technology or leave sarcastic meme reactions in team chats. Our candidate must stay strictly professional. They should not join in complaining, avoid leaving "thumbs up" or laughing reactions on toxic posts, and discuss any tech issues constructively with the designated technical contacts. Publicly bashing the tech stack leads to instant escalations and accusations of "lost trust".
    • Process-Oriented: The candidate should be okay with following bureaucratic routines, such as updating statuses daily in Jira, tracking time accurately in the client's system, and participating in daily stand-ups.
    • Inclusivity & Politeness: The client places a huge emphasis on soft skills and corporate culture. The candidate will be required to take mandatory security and inclusivity training, so a polite, empathetic, and culturally fitting personality is a strict requirement.
    • "Soft" Proactivity: No aggressive startup rushing - just polite, steady progress without ever pressuring or stressing the team(But proactivity is very welcome, just not aggressive)
Will be an advantage:
    • Experience in ERP systems and retail domain

To guarantee success here, we need a mature engineer who can navigate intense corporate politics, tolerate a slow, bureaucratic reality, requirements can be vague, and they might spend days just rewriting tests to satisfy a tech lead's subjective taste. Rather than rushing, we need a team player who proactively and tactfully clarifies unclear requirements with product owners, keeps tasks moving smoothly, and respects the client’s steady, measured pace.
